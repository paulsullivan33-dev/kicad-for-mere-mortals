# The Neighbours

KiCad is not the only way to design a
board. What the neighbours trade, stated
fairly — several are good tools, and one
of them may suit your constraints better.

| Tool | The shape of it | What it trades |
|---|---|---|
| **EasyEDA** | Browser-based, free tier, tied to a component supplier and a fab house | Zero install and a vast parts library with orderable part numbers, against your designs living in a vendor's cloud and the free tier's limits |
| **Fritzing** | Breadboard-first diagrams that grow into boards | The gentlest on-ramp there is — the breadboard view *is* the project — against a schematic and board capability that small projects outgrow, and a small charge for the download |
| **Eagle (in Fusion)** | The former hobbyist standard, now inside Autodesk's Fusion | A long history and many shared libraries, against subscription terms and board-size limits on the free edition that KiCad simply does not have |
| **Altium Designer** | The professional heavyweight on Windows | Depth no free tool matches — and pricing, licensing and weight that only make sense when boards are your job |
| **Horizon EDA** | Free and open source, like KiCad, with a parts-pool philosophy | A modern take on library management, against a smaller community and fewer shared designs to learn from |
| **Lepton EDA / gEDA** | The long-running Unix-native free tools | Scriptability and longevity, against an older interface and a schematic-first flow that newer users find austere |

Two questions sort most people:

- **Must the files live on your machine,
  in formats you control?** That is
  KiCad's home ground, with Horizon and
  Lepton. EasyEDA's convenience is real,
  and so is the lock-in it trades for it.
- **Is this a living or a hobby?** Altium
  earns its price in professional
  workflows. For everyone else its cost
  buys boards many times over at a fab
  house, designed in a free tool.

And one honest note about libraries you
will find online: the internet is full of
downloadable symbols and footprints for
every tool above. They are worth exactly
what chapter 10 says any footprint is
worth — trust them after you have checked
the pad numbers against the datasheet,
because the person who uploaded them is
not the person whose board fails.
